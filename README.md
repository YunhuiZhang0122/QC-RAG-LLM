A Reliability-Gated Cross-Domain Information Fusion Framework with Quantitatively Conditioned RAG-LLM for Personalized Rehabilitation Decision Support
The original data, experiments, and writing for this paper were all completed by Yunhui Zhang.

Abstract Older adults often present concurrent cognitive and motor impairments, but quantitative assessment and rehabilitation decision support are handled separately. This study proposes a reliability-gated cross-domain information fusion framework that connects cognitive-motor assessment with personalized rehabilitation decision support. A reliability-gated bidirectional domain-aware cross-attention medical Transformer (BDCMT) models heterogeneous demographic, cognitive, and motor measurements while preserving domain-specific representations and regulating cross-domain information exchange. A distributional ordinal loss further incorporates the ordered structure of impairment severity. Predicted grades, class-probability distributions, original measurements, deterministic scale summaries, demographic information, and attention-derived relevance cues are organized into a structured functional profile, which serves as an information-preserving interface for parallel retrieval from cognitive and motor rehabilitation knowledge bases. The structured profile, retrieved evidence, and predefined safety constraints are then integrated by a quantitatively conditioned retrieval-augmented large language model (QC-RAG-LLM) to generate personalized rehabilitation recommendations for clinician review. Across ten repeated runs on 977 community-dwelling older adults, BDCMT achieved mean accuracies of 0.8867 and 0.9036 for cognitive and motor impairment grading, respectively. In clinician evaluations of 40 stratified cases, the QC-RAG-LLM configuration received the highest ratings for safety, consensus, objectivity, reproducibility, and explainability. The framework provides a traceable pathway from quantitative assessment to evidence-informed personalized rehabilitation decision support.
Keywords Cross-domain information fusion; Cognitive-motor assessment; Ordinal learning; Retrieval-augmented generation; Large language models; Clinical decision support
